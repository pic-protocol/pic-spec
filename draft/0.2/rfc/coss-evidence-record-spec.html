Causally Ordered Security-State (COSS) Evidence Record

Abstract

This document is a subordinate specification of the PIC Specification 0.2. It defines the Causally Ordered Security-State (COSS) Evidence Record (CER): a protocol-neutral, transportable record that binds evidence from several security coordinates to one causal execution occurrence. COSS generalizes PIC from one authority coordinate to a finite product of ordered security coordinates while retaining PIC as the authority coordinate and preserving the foundational distinction between Proof of Relationship (PoR) and Proof of Continuity (PoC). A record may reference PIC authority-continuity evidence, information-flow evidence, execution-integrity evidence, geographic evidence, or other profile-defined evidence without absorbing the semantics of those systems.¶

The central rule is simple: one occurrence, one causal history, several independently verified coordinates. An authenticated digest reference proves that a record committed to exact artifact bytes; it does not prove that the referenced claim is true. Each required artifact remains subject to its own verifier, while the COSS record prevents valid evidence from unrelated executions from being mixed and presented as evidence of one effect.¶

1. Introduction

An effect can be authorized in one dimension and forbidden in another. A mail service may receive both a valid permission to send and bytes derived from confidential data. The permission answers what authority may continue. It does not answer where the bytes may flow.¶

COSS places those distinct security coordinates on the same causal execution [4] (Section 13). This document specifies the evidence boundary needed to carry that relationship between systems. It does not define the internal semantics or wire format of PIC, information-flow control (IFC), runtime attestation, or any other coordinate.¶

1.1. Requirements Notation

The key words "MUST", "MUST NOT", "REQUIRED", "SHALL", "SHALL NOT", "SHOULD", "SHOULD NOT", "RECOMMENDED", "NOT RECOMMENDED", "MAY", and "OPTIONAL" are to be interpreted as described in BCP 14 [2] (Section 13) [3] (Section 13) when, and only when, they appear in all capitals.¶

1.2. Scope

This specification defines:¶

  • the logical contents of a COSS Evidence Record;¶

  • the binding of coordinate evidence to one execution occurrence and its causal predecessor set;¶

  • execution integrity as an optional security coordinate, distinct from the evidence system that realizes it;¶

  • the distinction between Proof of Possession (PoP), Proof of Relationship (PoR), and Proof of Continuity (PoC);¶

  • ordered verification of ordinary continuations, joins, and explicit authorized relaxations; and¶

  • the limits of digest references and external evidence.¶

This specification does not define a serialization, signature suite, transport, evidence-retention system, PIC representation, IFC model, or product-specific mapping. Profiles define those choices. A mapping to an execution-evidence system is a profile of this specification, not a redefinition of PIC or COSS semantics.¶

1.3. Relationship to PIC

COSS is the multi-coordinate generalization of PIC. PIC remains the authority coordinate and the source of the authority-specific PoR, PoC, and non-expansion semantics. COSS places that coordinate beside other independently defined ordered coordinates and requires all of them to refer to one causal occurrence and one predecessor graph. COSS does not replace PIC, and PIC continuity alone does not establish continuity for the complete COSS product.¶

1.4. Planned Companion Specifications

This document is standalone and defines the record model and the semantic conditions for COSS PoR and PoC. A future companion COSS Prover and Verifier Specification (coss-prover-verifier-spec.md) may define concrete proof production, recursive graph verification, verifier-result objects, error semantics, and protocol bindings. A separate COSS-to-TRACE Profile (coss-trace-profile.md) may define a concrete TRACE mapping without making TRACE a dependency of COSS.¶

Those companion names describe planned work only. They do not identify published specifications, create conformance requirements, or alter the requirements of this document until separate documents are published and explicitly versioned.¶

2. One Effect, Several Questions

Alice authorizes reading her documents for a verification. Bob independently authorizes sending official email. Carol is an AI agent; in the accepted variant below, Carol acts as a Data Loss Prevention (DLP) service. Trent stores Alice's documents. Dave is a mail service whose rule forbids confidential data over email.¶

Alice -- read her documents, for verification --\
                                                 \
Bob   -- send official email --------------------> Carol -- send(bytes) --> Dave
                                                 /
Trent -- Alice's documents; classification HIGH /

Dave's rule: no confidential data by email
¶

The two permissions can be individually valid while the proposed effect remains inadmissible. Dave receives a permission and bytes:¶

send request
  permission: send email
  bytes:      52 65 6c 61 7a 69 6f 6e 65 ...

authority question: may this occurrence send email?
flow question:      may these bytes flow to this destination?
causal question:    which earlier occurrences produced this request and these bytes?
¶

Neither the bytes nor the send permission answers all three questions. A log entry with an actor and timestamp does not establish the causal relationship. Evidence is sufficient for this effect only when the relevant answers are bound to the same occurrence.¶

2.1. One Causal Axis

Let e be an execution occurrence and Pred(e) its represented immediate causal predecessors. Its represented history is the causal down-set Hist(e), not a wall-clock interval and not a second timeline per security mechanism [4] (Section 13) [7] (Section 13).¶

Alice intent ----> Trent release -----\
                                      > Carol send request ----> Dave effect
Bob intent --------------------------/
¶

Common ancestry, equal timestamps, the same executor, or co-residence in one process MUST NOT be treated as proof that two branches are the same continuation. A claimed predecessor edge requires profile-defined, causally sound evidence.¶

3. COSS Security State

For a finite set of coordinate profiles J, the security state at occurrence e is:¶

Sigma(e) = (sigma_j(e)) for every j in J
¶

Each profile j defines:¶

  • its state space;¶

  • its order <=_j, meaning "no more permissive than";¶

  • the behaviors admitted by a state; and¶

  • the evidence and verification procedure that establish its claims.¶

For an ordinary causal edge p -> e, COSS continuity requires:¶

PoR_COSS(p, e)  AND  sigma_j(e) <=_j sigma_j(p)  for every required j
¶

PIC is the authority coordinate of this construction. For set-valued PIC authority C, the order is set inclusion:¶

PoR_COSS(p, e)  AND  C(e) subset-of C(p)
¶

An IFC profile may provide a different state and order. COSS does not require distinct coordinates to use the same vocabulary, verifier, or artifact format. It requires their results to constrain the same occurrence.¶

At an ordinary multi-parent join, the child MUST be no more permissive than every parent in every required coordinate. For PIC authority:¶

C(e) subset-of intersection of C(p), for every p in Pred(e)
¶

A grant, authority composition, declassification, endorsement, or other non-conservative change MUST be represented as an explicit authorized relaxation. It MUST NOT be presented as an ordinary continuation.¶

3.1. Execution Integrity Coordinate

Execution integrity is an optional COSS coordinate describing which properties of the concrete execution a verifier has established. A profile may include properties such as the execution environment, measured workload, binding between that environment and the record-signing key, policy commitment, or transcript commitment. The coordinate is semantic and implementation-independent: an evidence format or project is not itself a coordinate.¶

An execution-integrity profile MUST define its state space, the order between states, the behaviors admitted at each state, the exact evidence required, the verifier and trust inputs, and the treatment of missing or unsupported evidence. It MUST distinguish producer claims from independently verified properties. Runtime attestation or a signature over an evidence envelope MUST NOT, by itself, be treated as proof that every claim inside the envelope is true.¶

TRACE is a candidate execution-evidence system for realizing such a profile [11] (Section 13). A TRACE Trust Record can commit to the program or workload named by the producer, runtime, policy, data classification, tool transcript, build provenance, and transparency information. TRACE version 0.2 authenticates the producer's record but does not by itself establish the truth of each contained claim. Its runtime-evidence work is currently a non-normative proposal for a future version and defines candidate attestation grades and external platform-verifier inputs [12] (Section 13).¶

A future COSS-to-TRACE profile may use a TRACE record as occurrence evidence and may map independently verified TRACE runtime results into the execution-integrity coordinate. Such a profile MUST identify the exact TRACE version and revision and MUST define same-occurrence binding, the coordinate order, admission rules, predecessor representation, and failure semantics. Merely naming TRACE, carrying a TRACE signature, or asserting a runtime platform does not establish conformance to this specification or to an execution-integrity profile.¶

4. COSS Proof of Relationship and Proof of Continuity

PoP, PoR, and PoC answer different questions:¶

Table 1
Primitive Question Evidence consequence
PoP Does the presenter control a key or artifact? Authenticates possession. It does not identify the causal occurrence.
COSS PoR Does this occurrence validly continue this specific predecessor on the shared causal graph? Establishes a profile-defined causal edge, same-occurrence binding, and the required relationship checks.
COSS PoC Does the whole represented execution preserve valid continuation across every required coordinate? Composes accepted COSS PoR edges, coordinate refinement, and authorized relaxations across the causal history.

PoP MAY protect a COSS record or a referenced artifact, but PoP alone MUST NOT be accepted as COSS PoR or COSS PoC. A digest chain establishes artifact linkage only. It establishes COSS continuity only when the applicable profiles also validate the causal relationships and coordinate rules.¶

PoR_COSS(p, e) holds only when the Verifier establishes that CER(e) identifies the exact authenticated CER(p) as a predecessor, that the claimed causal edge is valid under the selected profile, that every required evidence item is bound to the occurrence represented by e, and that all required profile-specific relationship checks succeed. When PIC is required, its authority-handoff relationship check contributes to PoR_COSS; that PIC check alone is not the complete COSS relationship result.¶

For an ordinary edge, the accepted step condition is:¶

Step_COSS(p, e) = PoR_COSS(p, e)
                  AND sigma_j(e) <=_j sigma_j(p) for every required j
¶

At a join, this condition applies to every represented immediate predecessor. For an authorized relaxation, the affected coordinate checks are replaced only by the separately verified relaxation rule; unaffected coordinates remain subject to the ordinary condition.¶

PoC_COSS(e) holds only when the resolved predecessor graph for Hist(e) is rooted, acyclic, and complete under the selected profile; every represented edge has an accepted COSS PoR; and every required coordinate transition is either ordinary and non-expanding or an explicitly authorized relaxation. A successful COSS PoC therefore entails PIC PoC when PIC is a required coordinate, but PIC PoC alone does not entail COSS PoC. Joint admission is a subsequent decision: Admit_COSS(e) additionally requires every coordinate selected by trusted policy to admit the committed behavior at the protected effect.¶

There is one predecessor graph. There is not an unrelated PIC chain, IFC chain, and execution-integrity chain later correlated by timestamp or actor name.¶

4.1. Evidence and Verification Results

A COSS Evidence Record is witness material. It carries or references the authenticated occurrence, predecessor, behavior, coordinate, and relaxation evidence on which a Verifier can operate. Possession, transport, signature validation, or schema validation of a record does not by itself establish COSS PoR or COSS PoC.¶

The Verifier derives separate results:¶

PoR_COSS(p, e) = accepted or rejected
PoC_COSS(e)    = accepted or rejected
Admit_COSS(e)  = accepted or rejected for the committed behavior
¶

A profile MAY define a serializable verifier-result object, but it MUST keep that result distinct from the evidence record and MUST identify the Verifier, trust inputs, profile versions, evaluation time, and exact record or graph evaluated.¶

5. COSS Evidence Record

A COSS Evidence Record CER(e) is an authenticated commitment with the following logical fields:¶

Table 2
Field Meaning
version The exact COSS evidence specification or profile version.
occurrence A commitment to the concrete execution occurrence.
policy An identifier or commitment for the trusted policy that selects required coordinates and verification rules.
transition root, ordinary, or authorized-relaxation.
predecessors Digest references to the exact authenticated predecessor records.
behavior A commitment to the request, message, or protected effect being judged.
evidence Typed references to occurrence, causal, or coordinate evidence.
relaxation For an authorized relaxation, references to its separate authorization and affected coordinates.

Every digest reference MUST identify the digest algorithm and the digest of the exact referenced bytes. The profile MUST define which bytes are hashed. A locator MAY accompany a digest but MUST NOT be trusted for identity or integrity.¶

An evidence entry declares one or more roles:¶

  • occurrence evidence describes what ran or which concrete request, message, or effect occurred;¶

  • causal evidence supports one or more claimed predecessor relationships; and¶

  • coordinate evidence supports a state, refinement, or admission result under a named coordinate profile.¶

One artifact MAY fulfill several roles. A runtime or provenance record is not automatically a security-state coordinate: it becomes coordinate evidence only under a profile that defines its state, order, admitted behavior, and verifier. For example, a TRACE record may be occurrence evidence without thereby making TRACE a coordinate; only an execution-integrity profile can assign independently verified TRACE results a coordinate state.¶

A coordinate-evidence entry MUST identify its coordinate and exact profile version. It MUST also carry or commit to the coordinate state established for the occurrence and reference the evidence from which that result is verified. The state MAY remain selectively disclosed if the profile supplies a proof sufficient for refinement and admission.¶

A root record has no predecessors. An ordinary or authorized-relaxation record has one or more distinct predecessors. The resolved predecessor relation MUST be acyclic. A profile MUST NOT infer a missing predecessor from physical time or list order.¶

5.1. Same-Occurrence Binding

Every required evidence entry MUST be bound to the record's occurrence. A profile MAY establish this in either of two ways:¶

  1. the evidence artifact directly commits to the same occurrence; or¶

  2. a trusted handoff boundary authenticates one record that commits together to the occurrence, its predecessor records, and the exact evidence-artifact digests.¶

Association performed later from timestamps, actor identifiers, workflow labels, or separately valid artifacts is insufficient. The binding MUST prevent an implementation from pairing valid PIC evidence from one execution with valid IFC or runtime evidence from another.¶

5.2. References Are Not Validation

An authenticated digest reference proves that the signer committed to exact artifact bytes. It does not prove that:¶

  • the referenced statement is true;¶

  • its issuer is trusted for the claimed role;¶

  • the artifact establishes PoR;¶

  • its coordinate state is correctly ordered; or¶

  • the behavior is admitted.¶

Each referenced artifact MUST therefore be resolved, integrity-checked, and independently validated by the verifier required by its declared profile. An unavailable required artifact causes verification to fail unless the selected profile defines an equivalent self-contained proof.¶

5.3. Illustrative Record

The following JSON is illustrative and is not a wire format or a registry of identifier strings:¶

{
  "version": "coss-evidence-record-0.2",
  "occurrence": {
    "profile": "mail-send-occurrence-v1",
    "digest": "sha-256:OCCURRENCE_DIGEST"
  },
  "policy": {
    "profile": "mail-boundary-policy-v1",
    "digest": "sha-256:POLICY_DIGEST"
  },
  "transition": "ordinary",
  "predecessors": [
    { "digest": "sha-256:PREDECESSOR_RECORD_DIGEST" }
  ],
  "behavior": {
    "profile": "mail-effect-v1",
    "digest": "sha-256:REQUEST_AND_BYTES_DIGEST"
  },
  "evidence": [
    {
      "roles": ["causal", "coordinate"],
      "coordinate": "pic-authority",
      "profile": "pic-continuity-profile",
      "state": { "digest": "sha-256:PIC_STATE_DIGEST" },
      "artifact": { "digest": "sha-256:PIC_EVIDENCE_DIGEST" }
    },
    {
      "roles": ["coordinate"],
      "coordinate": "information-flow",
      "profile": "ifc-provenance-profile",
      "state": { "digest": "sha-256:IFC_STATE_DIGEST" },
      "artifact": { "digest": "sha-256:IFC_EVIDENCE_DIGEST" }
    },
    {
      "roles": ["occurrence", "coordinate"],
      "coordinate": "execution-integrity",
      "profile": "execution-integrity-profile",
      "state": { "digest": "sha-256:EXECUTION_INTEGRITY_STATE_DIGEST" },
      "artifact": { "digest": "sha-256:RUNTIME_EVIDENCE_DIGEST" }
    }
  ]
}
¶

The record binds three external artifacts to one mail-send occurrence. It does not ask the execution-evidence system to compute PIC attenuation, and it does not ask PIC to decide IFC or execution integrity. Dave validates each required profile and then applies the joint admission rule.¶

5.4. Minimal Integration Contract

An execution-evidence system can support COSS Evidence Records without implementing PIC or IFC internally. Its profile needs to provide:¶

  1. an authenticated record for the concrete occurrence and behavior;¶

  2. digest references that form the represented predecessor graph;¶

  3. typed digest references to external coordinate evidence;¶

  4. same-occurrence binding for those references; and¶

  5. a way for the receiving verifier to invoke, or validate the result of, each required coordinate verifier.¶

The authority intersection remains a PIC operation. IFC admission remains an IFC operation. The execution-evidence system records and binds the facts on which those independent decisions rely.¶

6. Verification

A conformant verifier MUST perform the following checks in order:¶

  1. Record validation. Parse the record under the selected profile; reject unsupported versions, malformed fields, duplicate coordinate identifiers not explicitly allowed by profile, and unsafe resource consumption.¶

  2. Record authenticity. Validate the record's integrity, authenticating mechanism, trust anchor, algorithm policy, and profile-defined freshness or replay rules.¶

  3. Policy selection. Resolve the policy under local trusted configuration and determine the required coordinates, evidence profiles, and verification rules. The producer MUST NOT be able to select a weaker policy for the effect.¶

  4. Occurrence and behavior binding. Recompute or otherwise validate the commitments to the concrete request, message, or effect being judged.¶

  5. Reference integrity. Resolve every required artifact and verify its digest over the profile-defined exact bytes.¶

  6. Evidence validity. Apply the verifier and trust policy declared by every required evidence profile. A reference signature is not a substitute for this step.¶

  7. Causal validity. Validate that predecessor references are distinct, the resolved graph is acyclic, the declared predecessor set is complete under the selected profile, and the required PoR evidence is valid. Root, ordinary linear, ordinary join, and authorized-relaxation records MUST NOT be confused.¶

  8. Coordinate continuity. For an ordinary record, verify that every required coordinate is no more permissive than every applicable predecessor under that coordinate's declared order.¶

  9. Relaxation authorization. For an authorized relaxation, verify its separate authorization, the coordinates it may change, and the new bound. Unaffected coordinates remain subject to ordinary continuity.¶

  10. Joint admission. Before a protected effect, verify that every coordinate required by policy admits the committed behavior. Any required rejection rejects the effect.¶

An implementation MUST fail closed when a required coordinate, artifact, verifier, causal edge, or binding cannot be established. It MUST NOT silently reduce a multi-coordinate decision to the coordinates it happens to understand.¶

7. Worked Decision at Dave

7.1. Rejected Ordinary Path

For Carol's proposed mail effect, assume the evidence establishes:¶

PIC authority:       send-email is present
IFC provenance:      the bytes depend on Alice's HIGH documents
execution integrity: the required occurrence properties are independently established
destination policy:  external email is LOW
causal binding:      all results refer to this exact send occurrence
¶

The authority coordinate admits send-email. The IFC coordinate rejects the HIGH -> LOW flow. Dave therefore rejects the effect. PIC does not claim that Carol is physically unable to copy or transmit bytes through an unmediated path; it establishes that such an effect is not a valid admitted continuation. The claimed protection requires the mail effect to pass through Dave or another complete mediation boundary.¶

7.2. Accepted DLP Path

Suppose Carol does not initially hold send-email. Bob invokes Carol by combining the read-for-verification authority received from Alice with Bob's own send-email authority. That union is not an ordinary join. It MUST be represented as an explicit authorized composition whose COSS record has transition class authorized-relaxation.¶

Carol then evaluates the exact candidate output under the selected DLP or IFC profile. Two cases are distinct:¶

  1. If the verified output state already admits flow to the destination, no IFC relaxation is needed.¶

  2. If the output is derived from HIGH input and is released or sanitized for a LOW destination, the change MUST be an explicit authorized declassification. Its evidence MUST bind the input, exact output bytes, applicable policy, and declassification authorization.¶

The evidence graph makes both possible security boundaries visible:¶

Alice read ----\
                > [authorized composition: read + send] ---> Carol · DLP
Bob send  -----/                                             |
                                                             | verifies exact output
Trent data ------------------------------------------------->|
                                                             v
                                             [declassification, if required]
                                                             |
                                                             v
                                                     Dave mail effect
¶

The record presented to Dave binds at least:¶

behavior:                 digest of destination, message, and exact output bytes
predecessor:              digest of the authenticated predecessor record
PIC coordinate evidence:  digest of the authorized composition and authority state
IFC coordinate evidence:  digest of the DLP/IFC result for the exact output
relaxation evidence:      digest of declassification authorization, when required
occurrence evidence:      digest of the execution evidence for this handoff
¶

Dave accepts only if the composition is authorized, the PIC state admits send-email, the DLP or IFC result admits the exact output and destination, any required declassification is authorized, and every item is bound to the same occurrence. Without the required composition or declassification evidence, valid evidence in another coordinate does not repair the missing rule.¶

8. Conformance

A COSS Evidence Producer conforms to this specification when it creates authenticated records that bind the occurrence, policy, predecessors, behavior, evidence references, and transition class as required above.¶

A COSS Evidence Verifier conforms when it implements the ordered verification procedure, validates every required external profile independently, and fails closed on missing or unsupported required evidence.¶

A COSS Evidence Profile conforms when it defines the concrete serialization, authenticating mechanism, exact-byte digest rules, occurrence and behavior commitments, coordinate identifiers, coordinate orders, evidence verifiers, freshness rules, and required-coordinate policy needed to realize this specification.¶

Conformance to this document does not imply conformance to PIC, an IFC system, or any referenced evidence system. Those claims require independent conformance to the corresponding specifications.¶

9. Origins, Authorship, and Historical Record

This section is informative.¶

The Provenance Identity Continuity (PIC) Model was originally developed by Nicola Gallo as a formal framework for reasoning about causal authority propagation across distributed execution. Its development introduced Proof of Relationship (PoR), Proof of Continuity (PoC), authority non-expansion, relational attenuation, and event-level causal provenance as connected parts of the framework. COSS generalizes the carried security state from authority to a finite product of ordered security coordinates on the same causal occurrence. The PIC Specifications translate those formal results into technical requirements and are published and maintained by Nitro Agility S.r.l. as Specification Steward.¶

The name COSS abbreviates Causally Ordered Security-State. “Causally Ordered” identifies the single causal partial order shared by all represented security facts; “Security-State” identifies the product of ordered security coordinates carried on that structure. The name records how COSS was obtained from PIC: PIC remains the authority-continuity coordinate, while additional security-state coordinates are added without introducing a separate temporal or causal axis.¶

10. Security Considerations

The primary threat addressed by this document is evidence mix-and-match: combining valid evidence from different occurrences so that it appears to justify one effect. Same-occurrence binding and exact predecessor references are REQUIRED to prevent that substitution.¶

A digest authenticates bytes only through a separately trusted signature or channel and says nothing by itself about semantic truth. Weak, deprecated, ambiguous, or incorrectly canonicalized digest inputs can defeat the binding. Profiles MUST define exact-byte hashing and algorithm-agility policy.¶

Omitting a coordinate can change a security verdict. Required coordinates MUST therefore be selected by trusted policy, not by the producer. Unknown required coordinates and unavailable required evidence MUST fail closed.¶

Causal evidence can be incomplete or false. Claims that depend on complete causal ancestry additionally require sound causal evidence, representation of every in-scope cause, and mediation of every protected effect. This specification cannot protect causes or effects omitted from the modeled and mediated execution.¶

Replay, fan-out, freshness, revocation, trust-anchor compromise, and equivocation remain profile and deployment concerns. A valid historical record is not automatically valid for a new occurrence.¶

11. Privacy Considerations

Cross-coordinate evidence can expose principals, software identities, data classifications, locations, causal relationships, and stable execution identifiers. Profiles SHOULD use commitments, selective disclosure, scoped identifiers, and minimum necessary retention while preserving the evidence needed for the claimed security property. Digest references can still enable correlation when the same artifact is reused across domains.¶

12. Registry Considerations

This version defines no shared protocol registry and requests no external registry action. A future interoperability profile may define registries for coordinate identifiers, evidence roles, media types, digest algorithms, transition classes, or error values.¶

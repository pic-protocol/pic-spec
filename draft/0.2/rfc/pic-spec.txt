        Provenance Identity Continuity (PIC) Model Specification
                          pic-specification-02

Abstract

   This document is the *PIC Specification*: the entry point of the PIC
   specification set.  It defines the normative semantics of the
   *Provenance Identity Continuity (PIC) Model* and indexes the
   subordinate specifications that cover specific domains.

   The PIC Specification expresses the execution invariants of the PIC
   Model in normative form, without redefining, extending, or altering
   the underlying theoretical model.  In case of conflict, the *PIC
   Model* publications remain authoritative for the theoretical
   framework, while this *PIC Specification* is authoritative for
   normative requirements and conformance language.

1.  Introduction

   *  The *PIC Model* defines the foundational execution theory and
      invariants (see Section 9).

   *  The *PIC Specification* (this document and its subordinate
      specifications) defines the normative semantics of the PIC Model.

1.1.  Requirements Notation

   The key words "MUST", "MUST NOT", "REQUIRED", "SHALL", "SHALL NOT",
   "SHOULD", "SHOULD NOT", "RECOMMENDED", "NOT RECOMMENDED", "MAY", and
   "OPTIONAL" are to be interpreted as described in BCP 14 [2]
   (Section 9) [3] (Section 9) when, and only when, they appear in all
   capitals.

   Documents and implementations claiming conformance with PIC *MUST*
   faithfully preserve the invariants defined by the PIC Model as
   expressed by this Specification.  Anything that violates these
   invariants is *not PIC-compliant*, regardless of naming or intent.

   Subordinate specifications:

   *  MUST NOT redefine, extend, or alter the invariants of the PIC
      Model,

   *  MUST identify the version of this specification on which they
      depend,

   *  are canonical only in the version designated by the Specification
      Steward.

2.  Core Invariants

   This section is normative.

   A PIC-conformant specification, profile, or implementation MUST
   preserve all of the following invariants:

   1.  A root PIC Context of Authority establishes the upper authority
       bound for its lineage.

   2.  Every accepted non-root advancement MUST continue exactly one
       causal predecessor.

   3.  The selected profile MUST establish the required Proof of
       Relationship (PoR) relation between the predecessor and
       successor.



Gallo                         Informational                     [Page 3]

                            PIC Specification               October 2026


   4.  Successor authority MUST be no greater than predecessor authority
       under the selected profile's attenuation order.  For set-valued
       authority, successor authority is a subset of predecessor
       authority.

   5.  Authority from unrelated lineages MUST NOT be imported into a
       valid continuation.

   6.  A Proof of Continuity is the composition of valid hops that
       preserve these invariants.

   When a selected profile carries a relational executor envelope or
   execution contract, the predecessor envelope governs admission of the
   successor, the successor profile MUST satisfy it under sound
   evidence, and the carried successor envelope MUST preserve or
   strengthen it.  In logical notation, rho(i+1) => rho(i).  Withholding
   profile evidence MUST NOT widen admission [4] (Section 9).

   When a selected profile generalizes carried state to several ordered
   security coordinates, every ordinary transition MUST be non-expansive
   in every coordinate under that coordinate's declared semantic order.
   Any non-conservative coordinate change is an explicit authorized
   relaxation, not an ordinary continuation [5] (Section 9).

   These invariants define full PIC continuity.  A concrete profile or
   implementation MUST NOT claim a Proof of Continuity, the PIC
   confused-deputy exclusion result, or full PIC conformance merely
   because it authenticates artifacts, links checkpoint bytes, proves
   control of a key, or enforces non-expansion.  Its receiving predicate
   MUST also soundly establish the profile's concrete realization of the
   abstract PoR relation.  An implementation that establishes only a
   strict subset MAY describe those properties as a partial or artifact-
   linked assurance level, but MUST identify the missing refinement
   obligations and MUST NOT present that level as full PIC continuity
   [4] (Section 9).

   Claims about concrete protected effects require additional
   realization obligations: the modeled causal dependencies MUST be
   complete for the stated threat model, evidence for causal edges MUST
   be sound, and every protected effect in scope MUST be mediated
   against the authority state of its occurrence.  PIC continuity alone
   does not prove that hidden state, an omitted physical path, or an
   unmediated effect was represented [4] (Section 9) [5] (Section 9).

   The exactly-one-predecessor rule applies to an ordinary linear
   advancement.  Forks produce separate valid paths.  A profile that
   represents an ordinary multi-parent causal join MUST keep the child
   security state no more permissive than every parent; for set-valued



Gallo                         Informational                     [Page 4]

                            PIC Specification               October 2026


   authority, the child authority is therefore a subset of the
   intersection of the parent authorities.  A composition, grant,
   endorsement, or other non-conservative transition MUST be explicit
   and separately authorized and MUST NOT masquerade as an ordinary
   continuation [5] (Section 9).

4.  Origins, Authorship, and Historical Record

   This section is informative.

   The *Provenance Identity Continuity (PIC) Model* was originally
   developed by *Nicola Gallo* as a formal framework for reasoning about
   causal authority propagation across distributed execution.  Its



Gallo                         Informational                     [Page 7]

                            PIC Specification               October 2026


   development introduced Proof of Relationship (PoR), Proof of
   Continuity (PoC), authority non-expansion, relational attenuation,
   and event-level causal provenance as connected parts of the
   framework.  The PIC Specifications translate those formal results
   into technical requirements and are published and maintained by
   *Nitro Agility S.r.l.* as Specification Steward.

4.1.  Historical References

   The publicly verifiable research record is:

   *  *1 December 2025 — initial public PIC Model working paper.* _PIC
      Model — Provenance Identity Continuity for Distributed Execution
      Systems_, Zenodo record 17777421 [7] (Section 9).  That archived
      draft is retained for historical reference and is not an
      implementation specification.

   *  *5 December 2025 — authority-propagation analysis.* _Authority
      Propagation Models: PoP vs PoC and the Confused Deputy Problem_,
      Zenodo record 17833000 [6] (Section 9).

   *  *9 July 2026 — formal Proof-of-Continuity paper.* _Proof-of-
      Continuity: A Temporal Model for Authority Propagation in
      Distributed Systems and AI Agents_, arXiv:2607.08906 [1]
      (Section 9).

   *  *25 September 2026 — relational authority theory.* _Beyond Proof
      of Possession: A Relational Theory of Authority_, Zenodo record
      23102206 [4] (Section 9).

   *  *2 October 2026 — event-level causal closure.* _Full Provenance on
      the COSS Causal Axis: An Event-Level Closure of Causal Attribution
      and Confused-Deputy Exclusion_, Zenodo record 23102373 [5]
      (Section 9).

   The PIC Specification 0.2 family was first published on *15 July
   2026* and subsequently revised as the formal record developed.
   Published tags and immutable records identify the exact text
   applicable to a review or conformance claim.  Researchers and

5.  Implementation Status

   This section is informative and records implementation coverage at
   the time of this draft.

   PIC-X 0.2.3 implements the current centralized artifact and
   settlement subset described by the PIC Prover and Verifier
   Specification.  Its current assurance level is artifact-linked; it
   does not implement all obligations required for full semantic PoR,
   request/execution binding, causal attribution, revocation, or
   Sandboxed Execution.  Implementation coverage is evidence of running
   code, not a redefinition of PIC conformance.

6.  Security Considerations

   The security consequences of PIC depend on the concrete profile and
   its realization of PoR, non-expansion, freshness, request or
   execution binding, revocation, and complete mediation.
   Authentication of artifacts or possession of keys alone does not
   establish causal continuity.  The subordinate specifications define
   the detailed security requirements for their respective mechanisms.

7.  Privacy Considerations

   Continuity identifiers, causal relationships, authority contexts,
   executor evidence, and revocation state can enable correlation across
   services or execution steps.  Profiles and deployments should
   disclose only the evidence required by the receiving Verifier, scope
   stable identifiers to the smallest suitable trust domain, and define
   retention and access controls for continuity records.  Data
   minimization must not remove evidence required for a claimed security
   property.

8.  Registry Considerations

   This version defines no shared protocol registry and requests no
   external registry action.  A future wire-format or interoperability
   profile may define registries for identifiers, algorithms, evidence
   types, or error values.

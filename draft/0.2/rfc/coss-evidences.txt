                         COSS Temporal Evidence

Abstract

   This document is a subordinate specification of the PIC Specification
   0.2 (https://github.com/pic-protocol/pic-spec/blob/main/draft/0.2/
   pic-spec.md).  It defines *COSS Temporal Evidence*: a protocol-
   neutral way to bind evidence from several security coordinates to one
   causal execution occurrence.  A record may reference PIC authority-
   continuity evidence, information-flow evidence, runtime provenance,
   geographic evidence, or other profile-defined evidence without
   absorbing the semantics of those systems.

   The central rule is simple: *one occurrence, one causal history,
   several independently verified coordinates*. An authenticated digest
   reference proves that a record committed to exact artifact bytes; it
   does not prove that the referenced claim is true.  Each required
   artifact remains subject to its own verifier, while the COSS record
   prevents valid evidence from unrelated executions from being mixed
   and presented as evidence of one effect.

1.  Introduction

   An effect can be authorized in one dimension and forbidden in
   another.  A mail service may receive both a valid permission to send
   and bytes derived from confidential data.  The permission answers
   what authority may continue.  It does not answer where the bytes may
   flow.

   COSS places those distinct security coordinates on the same causal
   execution [4] (Section 13).  This document specifies the evidence
   boundary needed to carry that relationship between systems.  It does
   not define the internal semantics or wire format of PIC, information-
   flow control (IFC), runtime attestation, or any other coordinate.

1.2.  Scope

   This specification defines:

   *  the logical contents of a COSS Temporal Evidence Record;

   *  the binding of coordinate evidence to one execution occurrence and
      its causal predecessor set;

   *  the distinction between Proof of Possession (PoP), Proof of
      Relationship (PoR), and Proof of Continuity (PoC);

   *  ordered verification of ordinary continuations, joins, and
      explicit authorized relaxations; and

   *  the limits of digest references and external evidence.

   This specification does *not* define a serialization, signature
   suite, transport, evidence-retention system, PIC representation, IFC
   model, or product-specific mapping.  Profiles define those choices.
   A mapping to an execution-evidence system is a profile of this
   specification, not a redefinition of PIC or COSS semantics.

2.  One Effect, Several Questions

   Alice authorizes reading her documents for a verification.  Bob
   independently authorizes sending official email.  Carol is an AI
   agent; in the accepted variant below, Carol acts as a Data Loss
   Prevention (DLP) service.  Trent stores Alice's documents.  Dave is a
   mail service whose rule forbids confidential data over email.

Alice -- read her documents, for verification --\
                                                 \
Bob   -- send official email --------------------> Carol -- send(bytes) --> Dave
                                                 /
Trent -- Alice's documents; classification HIGH /

Dave's rule: no confidential data by email





Gallo                         Informational                     [Page 4]

                         COSS Temporal Evidence             October 2026


   The two permissions can be individually valid while the proposed
   effect remains inadmissible.  Dave receives a permission and bytes:

send request
  permission: send email
  bytes:      52 65 6c 61 7a 69 6f 6e 65 ...

authority question: may this occurrence send email?
flow question:      may these bytes flow to this destination?
causal question:    which earlier occurrences produced this request and these bytes?

   Neither the bytes nor the send permission answers all three
   questions.  A log entry with an actor and timestamp does not
   establish the causal relationship.  Evidence is sufficient for this
   effect only when the relevant answers are bound to the *same
   occurrence*.

2.1.  One Causal Axis

   Let e be an execution occurrence and Pred(e) its represented
   immediate causal predecessors.  Its represented history is the causal
   down-set Hist(e), not a wall-clock interval and not a second timeline
   per security mechanism [4] (Section 13) [7] (Section 13).

Alice intent ----> Trent release -----\
                                      > Carol send request ----> Dave effect
Bob intent --------------------------/

   Common ancestry, equal timestamps, the same executor, or co-residence
   in one process MUST NOT be treated as proof that two branches are the
   same continuation.  A claimed predecessor edge requires profile-
   defined, causally sound evidence.

3.  COSS Security State

   For a finite set of coordinate profiles J, the security state at
   occurrence e is:

   Sigma(e) = (sigma_j(e)) for every j in J

   Each profile j defines:

   *  its state space;

   *  its order <=_j, meaning "no more permissive than";

   *  the behaviors admitted by a state; and




Gallo                         Informational                     [Page 5]

                         COSS Temporal Evidence             October 2026


   *  the evidence and verification procedure that establish its claims.

   For an ordinary causal edge p -> e, COSS continuity requires:

   PoR(p, e)  AND  sigma_j(e) <=_j sigma_j(p)  for every required j

   PIC is the authority coordinate of this construction.  For set-valued
   PIC authority C, the order is set inclusion:

   PoR(p, e)  AND  C(e) subset-of C(p)

   An IFC profile may provide a different state and order.  COSS does
   not require distinct coordinates to use the same vocabulary,
   verifier, or artifact format.  It requires their results to constrain
   the same occurrence.

   At an ordinary multi-parent join, the child MUST be no more
   permissive than every parent in every required coordinate.  For PIC
   authority:

   C(e) subset-of intersection of C(p), for every p in Pred(e)

   A grant, authority composition, declassification, endorsement, or
   other non-conservative change MUST be represented as an explicit
   authorized relaxation.  It MUST NOT be presented as an ordinary
   continuation.

4.  PoP, PoR, and PoC in COSS Evidence

   PoP, PoR, and PoC answer different questions:

     +===========+=======================+===========================+
     | Primitive | Question              | Evidence consequence      |
     +===========+=======================+===========================+
     | *PoP*     | Does the presenter    | Authenticates possession. |
     |           | control a key or      | It does not identify the  |
     |           | artifact?             | causal occurrence.        |
     +-----------+-----------------------+---------------------------+
     | *PoR*     | Does this occurrence  | Establishes a profile-    |
     |           | validly continue this | defined causal edge and   |
     |           | specific predecessor? | binds the successor to    |
     |           |                       | the predecessor record.   |
     +-----------+-----------------------+---------------------------+
     | *PoC*     | Does the whole        | Composes accepted PoR     |
     |           | represented execution | edges and coordinate      |
     |           | preserve valid        | refinement across the     |
     |           | continuation?         | causal history.           |
     +-----------+-----------------------+---------------------------+

                                  Table 1

   PoP MAY protect a COSS record or a referenced artifact, but PoP alone
   MUST NOT be accepted as PoR or PoC.  A digest chain establishes
   artifact linkage only.  It establishes COSS continuity only when the
   applicable profiles also validate the causal relationships and
   coordinate rules.

   PIC PoC is the authority specialization.  COSS product continuity
   applies the same causal composition to every required ordered
   coordinate.  There is one predecessor graph; there is not an
   unrelated "PIC chain" and "IFC chain" later correlated by time or
   actor name.

5.  COSS Temporal Evidence Record

   A COSS Temporal Evidence Record TE(e) is an authenticated commitment
   with the following logical fields:

        +==============+=========================================+
        | Field        | Meaning                                 |
        +==============+=========================================+
        | version      | The exact COSS evidence specification   |
        |              | or profile version.                     |
        +--------------+-----------------------------------------+
        | occurrence   | A commitment to the concrete execution  |
        |              | occurrence.                             |
        +--------------+-----------------------------------------+
        | policy       | An identifier or commitment for the     |
        |              | trusted policy that selects required    |
        |              | coordinates and verification rules.     |
        +--------------+-----------------------------------------+
        | transition   | root, ordinary, or authorized-          |
        |              | relaxation.                             |
        +--------------+-----------------------------------------+
        | predecessors | Digest references to the exact          |
        |              | authenticated predecessor records.      |
        +--------------+-----------------------------------------+
        | behavior     | A commitment to the request, message,   |
        |              | or protected effect being judged.       |
        +--------------+-----------------------------------------+
        | evidence     | Typed references to occurrence, causal, |
        |              | or coordinate evidence.                 |
        +--------------+-----------------------------------------+
        | relaxation   | For an authorized relaxation,           |
        |              | references to its separate              |
        |              | authorization and affected coordinates. |
        +--------------+-----------------------------------------+

                                 Table 2

   Every digest reference MUST identify the digest algorithm and the
   digest of the exact referenced bytes.  The profile MUST define which
   bytes are hashed.  A locator MAY accompany a digest but MUST NOT be
   trusted for identity or integrity.

   An evidence entry declares one or more roles:

   *  *occurrence evidence* describes what ran or which concrete
      request, message, or effect occurred;

   *  *causal evidence* supports one or more claimed predecessor
      relationships; and

   *  *coordinate evidence* supports a state, refinement, or admission
      result under a named coordinate profile.




Gallo                         Informational                     [Page 8]

                         COSS Temporal Evidence             October 2026


   One artifact MAY fulfill several roles.  A runtime or provenance
   record is not automatically a security-state coordinate: it becomes
   coordinate evidence only under a profile that defines its state,
   order, admitted behavior, and verifier.

   A coordinate-evidence entry MUST identify its coordinate and exact
   profile version.  It MUST also carry or commit to the coordinate
   state established for the occurrence and reference the evidence from
   which that result is verified.  The state MAY remain selectively
   disclosed if the profile supplies a proof sufficient for refinement
   and admission.

   A root record has no predecessors.  An ordinary or authorized-
   relaxation record has one or more distinct predecessors.  The
   resolved predecessor relation MUST be acyclic.  A profile MUST NOT
   infer a missing predecessor from physical time or list order.

5.1.  Same-Occurrence Binding

   Every required evidence entry MUST be bound to the record's
   occurrence.  A profile MAY establish this in either of two ways:

   1.  the evidence artifact directly commits to the same occurrence; or

   2.  a trusted handoff boundary authenticates one record that commits
       together to the occurrence, its predecessor records, and the
       exact evidence-artifact digests.

   Association performed later from timestamps, actor identifiers,
   workflow labels, or separately valid artifacts is insufficient.  The
   binding MUST prevent an implementation from pairing valid PIC
   evidence from one execution with valid IFC or runtime evidence from
   another.

5.2.  References Are Not Validation

   An authenticated digest reference proves that the signer committed to
   exact artifact bytes.  It does *not* prove that:

   *  the referenced statement is true;

   *  its issuer is trusted for the claimed role;

   *  the artifact establishes PoR;

   *  its coordinate state is correctly ordered; or

   *  the behavior is admitted.



Gallo                         Informational                     [Page 9]

                         COSS Temporal Evidence             October 2026


   Each referenced artifact MUST therefore be resolved, integrity-
   checked, and independently validated by the verifier required by its
   declared profile.  An unavailable required artifact causes
   verification to fail unless the selected profile defines an
   equivalent self-contained proof.

5.4.  Minimal Integration Contract

   An execution-evidence system can support COSS Temporal Evidence
   without implementing PIC or IFC internally.  Its profile needs to
   provide:

   1.  an authenticated record for the concrete occurrence and behavior;

   2.  digest references that form the represented predecessor graph;

   3.  typed digest references to external coordinate evidence;

   4.  same-occurrence binding for those references; and

   5.  a way for the receiving verifier to invoke, or validate the
       result of, each required coordinate verifier.

   The authority intersection remains a PIC operation.  IFC admission
   remains an IFC operation.  The execution-evidence system records and
   binds the facts on which those independent decisions rely.

6.  Verification

   A conformant verifier MUST perform the following checks in order:

   1.   *Record validation.* Parse the record under the selected
        profile; reject unsupported versions, malformed fields,
        duplicate coordinate identifiers not explicitly allowed by
        profile, and unsafe resource consumption.

   2.   *Record authenticity.* Validate the record's integrity,
        authenticating mechanism, trust anchor, algorithm policy, and
        profile-defined freshness or replay rules.

   3.   *Policy selection.* Resolve the policy under local trusted
        configuration and determine the required coordinates, evidence
        profiles, and verification rules.  The producer MUST NOT be able
        to select a weaker policy for the effect.

   4.   *Occurrence and behavior binding.* Recompute or otherwise
        validate the commitments to the concrete request, message, or
        effect being judged.

   5.   *Reference integrity.* Resolve every required artifact and
        verify its digest over the profile-defined exact bytes.






Gallo                         Informational                    [Page 12]

                         COSS Temporal Evidence             October 2026


   6.   *Evidence validity.* Apply the verifier and trust policy
        declared by every required evidence profile.  A reference
        signature is not a substitute for this step.

   7.   *Causal validity.* Validate that predecessor references are
        distinct, the resolved graph is acyclic, the declared
        predecessor set is complete under the selected profile, and the
        required PoR evidence is valid.  Root, ordinary linear, ordinary
        join, and authorized-relaxation records MUST NOT be confused.

   8.   *Coordinate continuity.* For an ordinary record, verify that
        every required coordinate is no more permissive than every
        applicable predecessor under that coordinate's declared order.

   9.   *Relaxation authorization.* For an authorized relaxation, verify
        its separate authorization, the coordinates it may change, and
        the new bound.  Unaffected coordinates remain subject to
        ordinary continuity.

   10.  *Joint admission.* Before a protected effect, verify that every
        coordinate required by policy admits the committed behavior.
        Any required rejection rejects the effect.

   An implementation MUST fail closed when a required coordinate,
   artifact, verifier, causal edge, or binding cannot be established.
   It MUST NOT silently reduce a multi-coordinate decision to the
   coordinates it happens to understand.

7.  Worked Decision at Dave

7.1.  Rejected Ordinary Path

   For Carol's proposed mail effect, assume the evidence establishes:

   PIC authority:       send-email is present
   IFC provenance:      the bytes depend on Alice's HIGH documents
   destination policy:  external email is LOW
   causal binding:      all results refer to this exact send occurrence

   The authority coordinate admits send-email.  The IFC coordinate
   rejects the HIGH -> LOW flow.  Dave therefore rejects the effect.
   PIC does not claim that Carol is physically unable to copy or
   transmit bytes through an unmediated path; it establishes that such
   an effect is not a valid admitted continuation.  The claimed
   protection requires the mail effect to pass through Dave or another
   complete mediation boundary.

7.2.  Accepted DLP Path

   Suppose Carol does not initially hold send-email.  Bob invokes Carol
   by combining the read-for-verification authority received from Alice
   with Bob's own send-email authority.  That union is not an ordinary
   join.  It MUST be represented as an explicit authorized composition
   whose COSS record has transition class authorized-relaxation.

   Carol then evaluates the exact candidate output under the selected
   DLP or IFC profile.  Two cases are distinct:

   1.  If the verified output state already admits flow to the
       destination, no IFC relaxation is needed.

   2.  If the output is derived from HIGH input and is released or
       sanitized for a LOW destination, the change MUST be an explicit
       authorized declassification.  Its evidence MUST bind the input,
       exact output bytes, applicable policy, and declassification
       authorization.

   The evidence graph makes both possible security boundaries visible:

Alice read ----\
                > [authorized composition: read + send] ---> Carol · DLP
Bob send  -----/                                             |
                                                             | verifies exact output
Trent data ------------------------------------------------->|
                                                             v
                                             [declassification, if required]
                                                             |
                                                             v
                                                     Dave mail effect

   The record presented to Dave binds at least:

behavior:                 digest of destination, message, and exact output bytes
predecessor:              digest of the authenticated predecessor record
PIC coordinate evidence: digest of the authorized composition and authority state
IFC coordinate evidence: digest of the DLP/IFC result for the exact output
relaxation evidence:      digest of declassification authorization, when required
occurrence evidence:      digest of the execution evidence for this handoff

   Dave accepts only if the composition is authorized, the PIC state
   admits send-email, the DLP or IFC result admits the exact output and
   destination, any required declassification is authorized, and every
   item is bound to the same occurrence.  Without the required
   composition or declassification evidence, valid evidence in another
   coordinate does not repair the missing rule.

8.  Conformance

   A *COSS Temporal Evidence Producer* conforms to this specification
   when it creates authenticated records that bind the occurrence,
   policy, predecessors, behavior, evidence references, and transition
   class as required above.

   A *COSS Temporal Evidence Verifier* conforms when it implements the
   ordered verification procedure, validates every required external
   profile independently, and fails closed on missing or unsupported
   required evidence.

   A *COSS Evidence Profile* conforms when it defines the concrete
   serialization, authenticating mechanism, exact-byte digest rules,
   occurrence and behavior commitments, coordinate identifiers,
   coordinate orders, evidence verifiers, freshness rules, and required-
   coordinate policy needed to realize this specification.

   Conformance to this document does not imply conformance to PIC, an
   IFC system, or any referenced evidence system.  Those claims require
   independent conformance to the corresponding specifications.

9.  Origins, Authorship, and Historical Record

   This section is informative.

   The *Provenance Identity Continuity (PIC) Model* was originally
   developed by *Nicola Gallo* as a formal framework for reasoning about
   causal authority propagation across distributed execution.  Its
   development introduced Proof of Relationship (PoR), Proof of
   Continuity (PoC), authority non-expansion, relational attenuation,
   and event-level causal provenance as connected parts of the
   framework.  COSS generalizes the carried security state from
   authority to a finite product of ordered security coordinates on the
   same causal occurrence.  The PIC Specifications translate those
   formal results into technical requirements and are published and
   maintained by *Nitro Agility S.r.l.* as Specification Steward.

10.  Security Considerations

   The primary threat addressed by this document is *evidence mix-and-
   match*: combining valid evidence from different occurrences so that
   it appears to justify one effect.  Same-occurrence binding and exact
   predecessor references are REQUIRED to prevent that substitution.

   A digest authenticates bytes only through a separately trusted
   signature or channel and says nothing by itself about semantic truth.
   Weak, deprecated, ambiguous, or incorrectly canonicalized digest
   inputs can defeat the binding.  Profiles MUST define exact-byte
   hashing and algorithm-agility policy.

   Omitting a coordinate can change a security verdict.  Required
   coordinates MUST therefore be selected by trusted policy, not by the
   producer.  Unknown required coordinates and unavailable required
   evidence MUST fail closed.

   Causal evidence can be incomplete or false.  Claims that depend on
   complete causal ancestry additionally require sound causal evidence,
   representation of every in-scope cause, and mediation of every
   protected effect.  This specification cannot protect causes or
   effects omitted from the modeled and mediated execution.

   Replay, fan-out, freshness, revocation, trust-anchor compromise, and
   equivocation remain profile and deployment concerns.  A valid
   historical record is not automatically valid for a new occurrence.

11.  Privacy Considerations

   Cross-coordinate evidence can expose principals, software identities,
   data classifications, locations, causal relationships, and stable
   execution identifiers.  Profiles SHOULD use commitments, selective
   disclosure, scoped identifiers, and minimum necessary retention while
   preserving the evidence needed for the claimed security property.
   Digest references can still enable correlation when the same artifact
   is reused across domains.

12.  Registry Considerations

   This version defines no shared protocol registry and requests no
   external registry action.  A future interoperability profile may
   define registries for coordinate identifiers, evidence roles, media
   types, digest algorithms, transition classes, or error values.
